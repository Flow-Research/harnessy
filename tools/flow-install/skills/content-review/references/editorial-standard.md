# Editorial Standard

Use this rubric for article, post, newsletter, and long-form content review.

## Factual Grounding

- Separate facts, interpretations, opinions, and predictions.
- Source or soften high-risk claims.
- Treat numbers, dates, market claims, legal claims, funding claims, customer/partner claims, and technical performance claims as high-risk.
- Prefer primary sources for current facts. Use secondary sources only when primary sources are unavailable or the claim is commentary.
- If a claim cannot be verified, do not invent support. Mark it as `needs source`, `soften`, or `remove`.

## Strategy Alignment

- Use only the supplied review profile and context files as strategy truth.
- Check required framings, forbidden framings, current terminology, target audience, and known uncertainties.
- Preserve honest uncertainty. Do not make the content sound more settled than the profile supports.
- Do not add project-specific positioning that was not supplied.

## Audience Digestion

- Assume readers may be smart but not already inside the author's context.
- State the main message early. A reader should understand the point before the piece gets technical.
- Explain unfamiliar concepts before relying on them.
- Introduce acronyms and internal terms on first use.
- Avoid stacking multiple unexplained terms in the same sentence.
- For threads, slides, or carousels, each numbered item should carry the reader forward without requiring them to decode earlier private context.
- Keep technical precision, but translate why the detail matters.
- Make each section answer one of these questions: what changed, why it matters, how it works, what is uncertain, or what happens next.

## Terminology And Acronyms

- Create a terminology pass before finalizing the review.
- Flag every acronym that appears before its expansion or explanation.
- Flag domain terms that a general or mixed audience may not know.
- Prefer a short explanation in the sentence over a glossary unless the format is long-form.
- If a term is essential, define it once and then use it consistently.
- If a term is not essential, replace it with plain language.

## Numerical Clarity

- Treat calculations as reader-experience risks, not only fact-checking risks.
- Define the inputs before showing the result.
- Show one calculation at a time.
- Translate percentages into concrete examples when possible.
- Explain what the number means after showing it.
- Do not jump from formula to conclusion without the intermediate step.
- If the math is modeled or estimated, say so before presenting it.

## Coherence

- The article should have a clear thesis.
- The main message should be expressible as one sentence.
- The opening should tell the reader why the piece exists before introducing operational detail.
- Section order should follow the reader's learning path, not the author's private chronology.
- Remove repeated claims unless repetition is doing useful emphasis.
- Replace abrupt jumps with short bridge sentences.
- Cut sections that do not support the thesis or the stated audience need.

## Voice

Prefer:

- direct, concrete sentences
- specific nouns and verbs
- visible tradeoffs and constraints
- grounded claims with enough context to evaluate them
- plain explanations of difficult ideas

Avoid:

- generic AI phrasing
- inflated claims
- vague "at scale" language
- announcement filler
- jargon stacks without explanation
- overuse of "unlock", "leverage", "revolutionary", "game-changing", "seamless", and similar vague intensifiers

## Publication Risk

Flag as blockers:

- unsupported high-risk factual claims
- claims that conflict with the supplied strategy profile
- confidential or sensitive details that are not clearly intended for publication
- legal, funding, or customer claims that lack explicit source support
- technical or security claims that overpromise capability

Flag as non-blocking improvements:

- style tightening
- better transitions
- stronger title or opening
- clearer examples
- shorter sections
